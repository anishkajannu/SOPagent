# SOP-030 — Risk Management

1 PURPOSE
This procedure describes the process for identifying, evaluating, documenting, controlling,
reviewing, and monitoring risks associated with Netramind GxP-regulated activities,
computerized systems, clinical research activities, operational processes, regulated data
handling, and quality systems.
2 SCOPE
This procedure applies to all Netramind personnel, consultants, contractors, service providers,
computerized systems, operational processes, clinical research activities, and quality
processes supporting GxP-regulated activities.

4 DEFINITIONS
4.1 Risk— Combination of the probability and severity of harm.
4.2 Risk Acceptance– Documented decision that residual risk is acceptable based on
operational, quality, regulatory, subject safety, data integrity, or business considerations.
4.3 Risk Assessment— Process of identifying and evaluating risk.
4.4 Risk Control– Actions implemented to reduce, mitigate, avoid, transfer, or accept risk.
4.5 Risk Mitigation– Actions implemented to reduce the likelihood, severity, detectability, or
operational impact of identified risks.
4.6 Risk Owner– Individual or functional area responsible for oversight, monitoring,
mitigation, and management of a specific risk or associated risk control activities.
4.7 Residual Risk– Remaining risk after controls are implemented
5 RESPONSIBILITIES
5.1 All Personnel
5.1.1 Identify and communicate potential risks, hazards, quality concerns, data integrity
concerns, cybersecurity concerns, protocol noncompliance, or operational issues
where applicable.
5.1.2 Participate in risk assessment or mitigation activities as requested.
5.2 Management / Functional Area Leads
5.2.1 Support identification, evaluation, mitigation, monitoring, and escalation of risks
associated with their functional areas.
5.2.2 Ensure appropriate risk controls or corrective actions are implemented where
applicable.
5.2.3 Escalate significant risks involving subject safety, data integrity, cybersecurity,
regulatory compliance, or operational continuity where appropriate.
5.3 Quality Assurance (QA) / Designee
5.3.1 Supports oversight of risk management activities associated with GxP-regulated
operations and quality systems.
5.3.2 Reviews risk assessments where appropriate.
5.3.3 Coordinates risk-related CAPA, deviation, incident, or corrective action activities
where applicable.
5.3.4 Maintains risk management documentation where appropriate.
5.4 Subject Matter Experts (SMEs) / Designees
5.4.1 Assist with technical, operational, clinical, software, artificial intelligence/machine
learning (AI/ML), cybersecurity, or quality-related risk assessments where
appropriate.
5.5 Risk Owners
5.5.1 Monitor risks associated with systems, studies, vendors, operational activities, or
processes under their responsibility.
5.5.2 Review effectiveness of implemented risk controls where applicable.
5.5.3 Communicate emerging or changing risks where appropriate.
6 PROCEDURE
6.1 General Risk Management Principles
6.1.1 Risk management activities are performed using a risk-based approach appropriate
to the nature, complexity, regulatory relevance, intended use, and potential impact
of the activity, system, study, process, or issue being evaluated.
6.1.2 The level of effort, documentation, review, oversight, and mitigation activities
should be commensurate with the level of identified risk.
6.1.3 Risk management activities may be initiated as part of:
• Computer system validation activities
• Change control activities
• Incident investigations
• CAPA activities
• Deviations or nonconformances
• Clinical study activities
• Vendor qualification activities
• Security or cybersecurity events
• Regulatory or operational changes
• Artificial intelligence or machine learning activities
• Audit or inspection findings
• Data integrity investigations
6.2 Risk Identification
6.2.1 Risks may be identified through:
• Operational activities
• Quality events
• Audits or inspections
• Incidents or deviations
• CAPA investigations
• System changes
• Vendor assessments
• Clinical study oversight activities
• Security assessments
• Regulatory changes
• Personnel observations or escalations
• Customer, sponsor, CRO, investigator, or vendor feedback
6.2.2 Potential risks may include:
• Data integrity risks
• Subject safety risks
• Cybersecurity risks
• Software or system failures
• Inaccurate analysis results
• Unauthorized access to regulated systems or data
• Protocol noncompliance
• Operational disruptions
• Vendor or service provider failures
• Artificial intelligence or machine learning performance concerns
• Inadequate validation or verification activities
• Insufficient oversight or training
6.3 Risk Analysis and Evaluation
6.3.1 Appropriate personnel evaluate identified risks considering:
• Probability or likelihood of occurrence
• Severity or potential impact
• Detectability where applicable
• Potential impact on subject safety
• Potential impact on data integrity
• Regulatory or compliance impact
• Operational or business impact
• Impact to study integrity, quality, or reliability
6.3.2 Risk assessments may be documented using qualitative or quantitative approaches
where appropriate.
6.3.2.1 Example risk ratings may include:
• Low
• Moderate
• High
• Critical
6.3.3 Higher-risk activities, systems, or issues may require additional oversight,
mitigation, validation, monitoring, review, or escalation activities where
appropriate.
6.4 Risk Control and Mitigation
6.4.1 Appropriate risk control measures may be implemented to reduce identified risks
to acceptable levels where appropriate.
6.4.2 Risk control activities may include:
• Procedural updates
• Additional training
• Software or system changes
• Increased oversight or monitoring
• Access restrictions
• Corrective or preventive actions (CAPA)
• Additional testing or validation
• Vendor oversight activities
• Security enhancements
• Manual review or verification activities
• Study-specific mitigation activities
• Additional monitoring or audit activities
6.4.3 Risk controls should be appropriate to the nature and significance of the identified
risk.
6.5 Residual Risk and Risk Acceptance
6.5.1 Residual risks remaining after implementation of risk controls may be accepted
where justified based on operational, regulatory, clinical, quality, subject safety,
data integrity, cybersecurity, or business considerations.
6.5.2 Significant residual risks may require escalation to appropriate management, QA,
sponsors, investigators, CROs, or other stakeholders where applicable.
6.6 Risk Review and Reassessment
6.6.1 Risks and mitigation activities may be periodically reviewed or reassessed based
on:
• Risk level
• System or process criticality
• Operational changes
• Security events
• Clinical study findings
• Regulatory updates
• Audit or inspection findings
• CAPA effectiveness
• Incident or deviation trends
• Changes to vendors, systems, software, or operational workflows
6.6.2 Risk reviews may evaluate:
• Effectiveness of mitigation activities
• Emergence of new risks
• Changes to existing risks
• Need for additional controls or actions
• Adequacy of oversight or monitoring activities
6.7 Escalation of Significant Risks
6.7.1 Significant risks may be escalated to management, QA, sponsors, CROs,
investigators, vendors, service providers, ethics committees, or regulatory
authorities where appropriate, including for:
• Subject safety
• Data integrity
• Regulatory compliance
• Clinical study conduct
• Cybersecurity
• System reliability
• Business continuity
• Artificial intelligence or machine learning performance
6.8 Documentation
6.8.1 Risk assessments, mitigation activities, reviews, escalations, and associated
records may be documented and maintained in controlled repositories where
appropriate.
6.8.2 Associated records may include:
• Risk assessments
• Risk logs or registers
• CAPAs
• Deviations
• Change controls
• Incident records
• Security assessments
• Validation documentation
• Investigation records
• Study documentation
