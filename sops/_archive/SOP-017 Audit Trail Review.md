# SOP-017 — Audit Trail Review

1. PURPOSE
This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) describes the requirement and process for periodic review
of audit trails generated by GxP regulated computerized systems to assure appropriate
traceability of data transformation relevant to data integrity in the conduct of clinical trials, and
may be supplemented by separate procedures for commercial or post-market systems, as
applicable.
2. SCOPE
This SOP applies to audit trail review of GxP computerized systems used by Netramind to generate,
process, or report clinical trial data.
Netramind may delegate tasks from this SOP to a service provider if Netramind has determined their
SOPs are acceptable for use. However, Netramind retains overall responsibility for those
delegated activities (ICH E6[R3], Section II.10.1).

4. DEFINITIONS
4.1. Audit Trail— Metadata records that allow the appropriate evaluation of the course of events by
capturing details on actions (manual or automated) performed relating to information and data
collection and, where applicable, to activities in computerized systems. The audit trail should
show activities, initial entry, and changes to data fields or records, by whom, when and, where
applicable, why. In computerized systems, the audit trail should be secure, computer-
generated, and time stamped.
4.2. Computerized Systems Validation (CSV)— A process of establishing and documenting that
the specified requirements of a computerized system can be consistently fulfilled from
design until decommissioning of the system or transition to a new system. The approach to
validation should be based on a risk assessment that takes into consideration the intended
use of the system and the potential of the system to affect trial participant protection and
the reliability of trial results
4.3. Data Acquisition Tool (DAT)—A paper or electronic tool designed to collect data and associated
metadata from a data originator in a clinical trial according to the protocol and to report the
data to the sponsor. The data originator may be a human (e.g., the participant or trial staff), a
machine (e.g., wearables and sensors), or a computer system from which the electronic
transfer of data from one system to another has been undertaken (e.g., extraction of data from
an electronic health record or laboratory system). Examples of DATs include but are not limited
to CRFs, interactive response technologies, clinical outcome assessments, including patient-
reported outcomes (PROs) and wearable devices, irrespective of the media used.
4.4. Data Integrity—Data integrity includes the degree to which data fulfill key criteria of being
attributable, legible, contemporaneous, original, accurate, complete, secure and reliable such
that data are fit for purpose.
4.5. Human Readable Format—Audit trail data that can be interpreted and reviewed by a user
without requiring specialized system-level access.
4.6. Metadata— The contextual information required to understand a given data element.
Metadata is structured information that describes, explains, or otherwise makes it easier to
retrieve, use, or manage data to allow for the appropriate evaluation of the trial conduct.
4.7. Raw Data—Factual recorded information obtained as a result of original observations or
activities, which has not been copied or transcribed. This term is synonymous with original
data.
4.8. Signature—A unique mark, symbol, or entry executed, adopted, or authorized by an
individual, in accordance with applicable regulatory requirements and/or practice to show
expression of will and allow authentication of the signatory (i.e., establish a high degree of
certainty that a record was signed by the claimed signatory). A signature may be physical
or electronic
4.9. Source Records— Original documents or data (which includes relevant metadata) or certified
copies of the original documents or data, irrespective of the media used. This may include trial
participants’ medical/health records/notes/charts; data provided/entered by trial participants
(e.g., electronic patient-reported outcomes (ePROs)); healthcare professionals’ records from
pharmacies, laboratories, and other facilities involved in the clinical trial; and data from
automated instruments, such as wearables and sensors.
4.10.Subject Matter Expert (SME)—An individual designated by management as an expert in a
particular area based on attributes such as education, training, and experience.
5. RESPONSIBILITIES
5.1. Business Process Owner (BPO)
5.1.1. Identify the regulatory and business requirements that the computerized system must
comply with based on risk assessments.
5.1.2. Review and approve audit trail specifications.
5.1.3. Resolve audit trail issues with IT Quality Assurance (QA).
5.1.4. Ensure that the audit trail function is always enabled and not modifiable in GxP-
regulated computer systems.
5.1.5. Coordinate with IT and/or System Owner to designate qualified audit trail reviewers.
5.1.6. Approve Audit Trail Reports.
5.2. Designated Audit Trail Reviewer (may include System Administrator or trained personnel)
5.2.1. Performs audit trail reviews.
5.3. System Owner (SO)
5.3.1. Ensure system users have requisite training for system use.
5.3.2. Approve system access to authorized system users.
5.3.3. Approve Audit Trail Reports.
5.3.4. Coordinate with the Business Process Owner to designate qualified audit trail
reviewers.
5.4. Quality Assurance (QA)
5.4.1. Provides Audit Trail oversight.
5.4.2. Approves Audit Trail Report.
6. PROCEDURE
6.1. Identifying Regulatory and Business Requirements for Computerized Systems
6.1.1. The BPO conducts a risk assessment of the computerized system and associated
platforms to ensure it meets the intended use for business needs.
6.1.2. For systems which generate, store, report on data collected for GxP purpose, the BPO
ensures that the system(s) complies with applicable regulatory requirements, in
accordance with SOP-015 21CFR Part 11 and SOP-016 Data Integrity.
6.1.3. Considerations during the computerized system risk assessment should include:
6.1.3.1. Ensuring that metadata is captured with acquired data
6.1.3.2. Automated data validation checks
6.1.3.3. User account management
6.1.3.4. Traceability of data changes
6.1.3.5. Integrated workflows
6.1.3.6. Automated capture of date and time during data capture and transfers
6.1.3.7. Periodic review of trial-specific data, Audit Trails, and other metadata
6.1.3.8. Documentation of change controls
6.1.4. The BPO documents and communicates the risk assessment results with the IT System
Owner(s) and System Administrator(s).
6.2. Establishing Audit Trail Items for a Computerized System
6.2.1. The Business Process Owner (BPO), System Owner, and QA jointly define audit trail
scope, review items, and frequency, based on the risk category assigned to each
computerized system.
6.2.2. The extent and nature of periodic review of audit trails should be risk-based:
6.2.2.1. Review should be adapted to meet the trial design, phase, and complexity of
the data, as defined by critical data elements and processes.
6.2.2.2. Review should focus on critical systems that process critical data elements and
the relevant metadata.
6.2.3. Audit trails and relevant metadata must be recorded in such a way that is decipherable,
changes should be supported by source data around the time of the original entry.
6.2.4. The BPO, System Owner, and QA use the results from the risk assessment to establish
audit review items, including the review frequency, based on the risk category assigned
to each computerized system.
6.2.4.1. System or Process Owner determines audit trails needs based on system and
study specific requirements.
6.2.4.2. QA defines the requirements for audit trail transactions to comply with ICH
standards.
6.2.4.3. QA determines the requirements for audit trail reviews, including relevant
metadata, and frequency of reviews.
6.2.5. End-users assigned to perform periodic reviews of each system and/or each clinical trial
are trained and understand the requirements for reviewing and documenting the
results.
6.3. Conducting an Audit Trail Review
6.3.1. Audit trail reviews should focus on identifying unauthorized changes, unexpected data
modifications, and system misuse.
6.3.2. The BPO and SO designate a qualified audit trail reviewer to conduct periodic Audit Trail
reviews, based on each computerized system and for each relevant clinical trial.
6.3.3. The designated System Administrator and/or Audit Trail Reviewer schedules an Audit
Trail periodic review based on the frequency defined in the risk assessment.
6.3.4. The following requirements and methods may be utilized:
6.3.4.1. Ensure that audit trails are available in Human Readable Format
6.3.4.2. Audit trail reviews are conducted either manually or using software tools such
as SQL queries, tag filters, etc. based on the established system requirements.
6.3.4.3. Audit Trail review should evaluate activities, initial entries, and changes to data
fields or records, by whom, when and, where applicable, why.
6.3.4.4. Additional information to clarify anomalies in the audit trail may be required,
such as study team list, data handling guidelines, etc. which the audit trail
reviewer should be informed prior to the review. The reviewer can also
request additional clarifications from the SO and/or study SMEs.
6.3.4.5. Document each finding with sufficient details to reference the discrepancies.
6.3.4.6. Verification that changes to critical data are appropriately justified and
documented.

6.4. Audit Trail Periodic Review Report
6.4.1. Documentation may be maintained in a structured report, log, or tracking system,
provided traceability is maintained.
6.4.2. The System Administrator and/or Reviewer captures the results of the Audit Trail
periodic review in a report (Appendix 1).
6.4.3. Identified issues and/or unusual incidents are escalated to appropriate Functional Area
management personnel and QA for resolution.
6.4.3.1. Each observation is assigned a unique Audit Trail Review (ATD) ID number (i.e.,
ATD-001).
6.4.3.2. Additional details, e.g., record numbers, date of event, captured image of
record can be supplemented to better assess the finding(s).
6.4.3.3. If systematic observations are noted, the report should include
recommendations for further investigation or increased review scope (i.e.,
frequency, types of data, sampling size) to fully assess the root cause of the
findings.
6.4.4. The System Administrator and/or Reviewer distribute the completed Audit Trail Review
Report to the BPO, System Owner, and QA for review and approval. Additional
stakeholders may also be provided with the report for review, e.g., Functional Area
Management, SMEs, etc.
6.4.5. The BPO, System Owner, and QA review and approve the completed Audit Trail Report.
6.4.5.1. If additional review or corrective actions are required, the BPO, SO and QA
determine whether further investigation, corrective action, or escalation is
required.
6.4.5.2. The recommendations are communicated to relevant SO, SMEs, and/or
Functional Area Management for further resolution.
6.4.6. The approved Audit Trail Report is filed and archived in the relevant system accessible
to the BPO, System Owner, and QA.
6.4.7. A copy of the Audit Trail Report can be filed in the study TMF, if the report was
generated for a study-specific system and/or process.
6.5. Compliance with Periodic Reviews
6.5.1. The BPO, System Owner and QA maintains a record of scheduled and completed audit
trail reviews for each computer system and/or study and are responsible for adhering
to the scheduled periodic review as required in the initial risks assessment, e.g.,
annually.
6.5.2. If the periodic review frequency changes, due to system status, study closure, etc. the
BPO, System Owner and QA ensure the changes are captured on the tracker, along with
the rationale for updating the periodic review frequency or requirements.
