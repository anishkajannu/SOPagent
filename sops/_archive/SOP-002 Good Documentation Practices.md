# SOP-002 — Good Documentation Practices

1 PURPOSE
This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) describes the minimum requirements for recording
information according to Good Documentation Practices, to ensure that all records and data meet the
regulatory principles for ALCOAC++ (Attributable, Legible, Contemporaneous, Original, Accurate,
Complete, Consistent, Enduring, Available, and Traceable).
2 SCOPE
This SOP applies to all employees, contractors and service providers involved in generating and
documenting records and data including paper and/or electronic version of controlled forms, reports,
source data, and any other records or media produced in support of any GxP relevant activity at
Netramind.

4 DEFINITIONS
4.1 Accurate—Documentation that is authentic, thorough, and clear.
4.2 Approved By—Signature of a qualified individual (such as a supervisor) indicating that the
information documented is complete, accurate, and acceptable.
4.3 Attributable—Data and documentation that is traceable to a person and date.
4.4 Backdating (Pre-dating)—Recording of a signature and/or date that is before the actual date that
the document was initialed, signed, or an activity occurred. Backdating does not comply with the
ALCOA ++ principle of data being contemporaneous and is therefore unacceptable.
4.5 Certified Copy— A copy (irrespective of the type of media used) of the original record that has
been verified (i.e., by a dated signature or by generation through a validated process) to have
the same information as the original, including relevant metadata, where applicable.
4.6 Contemporaneous—Data and documentation associated with an activity that is recorded as the
activity occurs.
4.7 Controlled Documents—Documents (i.e., Quality Manual, SOPs, Work Instructions [WIs], Forms
[FMs], etc.) required by the Quality Management System (QMS) that are controlled throughout
the document lifecycle by identification, storage, protection, version control, retrieval, retention,
and disposition.
4.8 Data Acquisition Tool (DAT) – A paper or electronic tool designed to collect data and associated
metadata from a data originator in a clinical trial according to the protocol and to report the data
to the sponsor. The data originator may be a human (e.g., the participant or trial staff), a
machine (e.g., wearables and sensors) or a computer system from which the electronic transfer
of data from one system to another has been undertaken (e.g., extraction of data from an
electronic health record or laboratory system). Examples of DATs include but are not limited to
CRFs, interactive response technologies (IRTs), clinical outcome assessments (COAs), including
patient-reported outcomes (PROs) and wearable devices, irrespective of the media used.
4.9 Data Integrity – Data integrity includes the degree to which data fulfil key criteria of being
attributable, legible, contemporaneous, original, accurate, complete, secure and reliable such
that data are fit for purpose.
4.10Essential Records – Essential records are the documents and data (and relevant metadata), in
any format, associated with a clinical trial that facilitate the ongoing management of the trial
and collectively allow the evaluation of the methods used, the factors affecting a trial and the
actions taken during the trial conduct to determine the reliability of the trial results produced
and the verification that the trial was conducted in accordance with GCP and applicable
regulatory requirements.
4.11Falsification—Intentionally recording incorrect or inaccurate data.
4.12GxP Records— Records associated with the development, manufacture, packaging, testing,
marketing, and distribution of the active substance (i.e., API), pharmaceutical product (i.e., drug
product), or medical device that are used for clinical and commercial purposes. This also includes
registration and regulatory records for both clinical and commercial products.
4.13Indelible—Cannot be washed away, erased, easily removed, or smudged. Ink that runs when
wet, as with felt tip pens, is not indelible.
4.14Legible—Data and documentation must be clear enough to read.
4.15Metadata— The contextual information required to understand a given data element. Metadata
is structured information that describes, explains or otherwise makes it easier to retrieve, use or
manage data. For the purpose of this guideline, relevant metadata are those needed to allow the
appropriate evaluation of the trial conduct.
4.16Original record/data— Data as the file or format in which it was originally generated, preserving
the integrity (accuracy, completeness, content and meaning) of the record, e.g., original paper
record of manual observation, or electronic raw data file from a computerized system. (Source:
Data Integrity Definitions and Expectations (Revision 1), Jan, 2015, MHRA)
4.17Performed By—Initials or signature of the person executing an operation or task.
4.18Postdating— Recording of a signature and date with a date later than the execution date.
Postdating does not comply with the ALCOAC++principle of data being contemporaneous and is
therefore unacceptable.
4.19Raw Data— Factual recorded information obtained as a result of original observations or
activities, which has not been copied or transcribed. This term is synonymous with original data.
4.20Reviewer—An individual who checks over a document for accuracy to confirm that all required
supporting data, references, signatures, etc. are present.
4.21Signature —A unique mark, symbol or entry executed, adopted or authorized by an individual, in
accordance with applicable regulatory requirements and/or practice to show expression of will
and allow authentication of the signatory (i.e., establish a high degree of certainty that a record
was signed by the claimed signatory). A signature may be physical or electronic.
4.22Source Records— Original documents or data (which includes relevant metadata) or certified
copies of the original documents or data, irrespective of the media used. This may include trial
participants’ medical/health records/notes/charts; data provided/entered by trial participants
(e.g., electronic patient-reported outcomes (ePROs)); healthcare professionals’ records from
pharmacies, laboratories and other facilities involved in the clinical trial; and data from
automated instruments, such as wearables and sensors.
4.23True Copy—An exact copy of an original record, which may be retained in the same or different
format in which it was originally generated, e.g., a paper copy of a paper record, an electronic
scan of a paper record, or a paper record of electronically generated data.
4.24Verified By—Initials or signature of a person examining a task, document, or record to confirm its
accuracy and completeness.
5 RESPONSIBILITIES
5.1 All Netramind employees, contractors and service providers (SPs)
5.1.1 Completes, reviews, and/or approves handwritten, printed, and electronic records in
compliance with standard procedures, plans and GDP.
6 PROCEDURE
6.1 Employee Signatures
6.1.1 Personnel must be uniquely identifiable in all records via name, signature, or system-
based authentication.
6.2 Glossary of Terms
6.2.1 Definitions and abbreviations used across documents must be consistent and defined
within the documents in which they are being used.
6.2.2 If Netramind maintains a Glossary, terms and definitions must be current per regulatory
guidance, regulations and industry standards.
6.3 General Data Recording Guidelines on Paper Source
6.3.1 All data must be attributable, legible, contemporaneous, original, accurate, complete,
consistent, enduring, available, and traceable (ALCOAC++).
6.3.2 An individual cannot perform and approve or verify the same task.
6.3.3 All information and data must be recorded in real time (i.e., when the activity/ task was
performed).
6.3.4 All manual data entries must be made by the employee who performed the task. If the
original member is not able to make entries and another member has the document on
behalf of the original member, a note should be clearly indicated with accompanying
initials and date.
6.3.5 All signatures/ initials must be accompanied by a date. Assigning one date for all
signatures/ initials on a single page is not acceptable.
6.3.6 Entries must be made immediately after completion of a step directly onto the original
written document. If data or other information were omitted and recorded later,
employees must:
6.3.6.1 Add a justification explaining why the data was added later,
6.3.6.2 Reference supporting evidence, if any, that authenticates the late entry, and
6.3.6.3 Add their initials and the date on which the late entry was added.
6.3.7 Backdating and postdating are not permitted. If an error is discovered, use the current
date on which the correction is made. Clearly indicate who is making the correction
(initials), what occurred, how and where the information was recovered, and why
(providing a clearly written, concise, easily understood, and scientifically sound
explanation). A manager’s signature may be needed to verify the correction.
6.3.8 Unless specifically required by a protocol or SOP, there should only be one set of original
study data (for a clinical study).
6.4 Guidelines for data recording using Data Acquisition Tools
6.4.1 Data acquisition tools (DATs) should be documented as being fit for purpose.
6.4.1.1 For DATs developed by Netramind, respective software development and testing
procedures must be adhered to, and then the DAT must be validated in
accordance with SOP-014 Computer System Validation.
6.4.1.2 For vendor-supplied DATs, those vendors must first be qualified per SOP-026 CSP
Selection, Qualification, and Oversight.
a. An appropriate risk-based validation approach must be undertaken for
vendor-supplied DATs as per SOP-014 Computer System Validation.
6.4.2 Procedures specific to each DAT must be developed which govern their study-specific
configuration and testing, defined user roles and periodic reviews of user access,
requirement to define a risk-based audit trail review approach, and appropriate use of
the DAT.
6.4.2.1 If the data captured in the DAT can be unblinding, a blind maintenance plan
must be created.
6.4.3 DATs must:
6.4.3.1 Have audit trail functionality that is exportable and legible.
6.4.3.2 Only capture data and information required by the protocol.
6.4.3.3 Be validated and then configured for study-specific use prior to the start of the
study.
6.4.3.4 Have a training program for all user types to enable appropriate use of the DAT.
6.4.4 The above can be delegated to a third-party; however, the third-party must be able to
meet all requirements of Netramind for use of DATs.
6.5 Data Correction
6.5.1 The use of correction materials such as correction fluid or tape that obliterates original
information is not permitted. The original entry must remain legible or visible.
6.5.2 A comment explaining the reason for the correction must be included in the Comment
section or elsewhere on the same page as the correction.
6.5.3 If corrections are made after the date of the original data entry, the Department Manager
reviews and approves justification for the changes. Document an explanation about the
correction.
6.5.4 If an individual fails to sign and date information on an entry and a correction is made at
a later date, sign and date the entry on the date the correction is made and indicate the
original entry date. Include an explanation for the correction.
6.5.5 Corrections must be made by the individual who performed the documentation, with the
following exceptions:
6.5.5.1 Retrievable information can be corrected by anyone, such as entries made on
summaries from raw test data.
6.5.5.2 A correction to a document made by an individual who is no longer employed by
Netramind or is not available can be performed by management.
6.5.6 If an original document must be rewritten due to damage, attach the original document.
Provide an explanation for the rewritten document.
6.5.7 Corrections made after a record is approved require an explanation and must be
approved by the original approvers of the record or their equivalent role.
6.6 Concealing Private/ Confidential Information on Source Documents
6.6.1 Personal/identifiable information must be appropriately protected or redacted in
accordance with data privacy requirements.
6.7 Clarifying Information
6.7.1 Clarification is required when information on a document is illegible, not clear, or can
lead to misinterpretation.
6.7.2 The individual entering clarification details must initial and date the document.
6.8 Completeness of Records
6.8.1 When a document or record requires an entry to provide evidence of activity completion
and the activity was performed but not documented, include an explanation of why there
was an omission.
6.9 Transcribing Data
6.9.1 Where data is transcribed, the transcription must be accurate, attributable, and verified.
Original records must be retained or referenced to maintain traceability.
6.10Recreation of Lost Records
6.10.1 All instances of original lost records must be documented as a deviation per SOP-006,
Deviation Management, regardless of whether content could be recreated from other
sources.
6.11Handling Fraud in Documentation
6.11.1 Fraudulent documentation practices are prohibited and should result in disciplinary
action and/or termination of employment. SOP-029 Management of Potential Fraud and
Serious Misconduct provides further details.
6.11.2 Fraudulent documentation practices include, but are not limited to:
• Altering data to meet acceptance criteria.
• Unqualified staff performing a task.
• Obscuring original data
• Forging signatures
• Backdating data entries
• Back-entering data with no traceability
• Disposing of unused data
• Entering data results when testing was not performed.
• Signing and entering dates and times prior to or after activities have been
performed.
• Destroying original data without approval
• Verifying a step, task, calculation, or other entry without observation
